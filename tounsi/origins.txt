3aslema|Short for "3la slema", "(you came) in safety": the same wish as Arabic "salam" (peace). Tunisia's own hello — Algerians and Moroccans say "salam" or "ahlan" instead, so this one word marks you as Tunisian.
bislema|"Bi-s-slema", "with safety": Arabic "salama", wholeness and peace. The two ends of a Tunisian conversation share one root: you arrive 3aslema and leave bislema.
3aychek|Literally "may (God) keep you alive", from 3ach, to live. A blessing turned into "thanks" and "please" — a very Tunisian word; elsewhere in the Arab world people say shukran.
sam7ni|"Forgive me", from sma7, to forgive, pardon, allow. Classical Arabic samaha, generous forgiveness; "masmou7" (allowed) is the same root. Tunisians use it for "sorry" and "excuse me" alike.
chnowa|From "ech" (what) + "houwa" (he/it): "what is it?" The feminine is chniya, "what is she?" Each Maghreb dialect built its own "what": Moroccans say chnou, Egyptians eh.
chkoun|"Ech" (what) + "ykoun" (would be): "what would it be?" became "who?" Shared with Algeria and Morocco; Classical Arabic says "man", which Tunisians never use for "who".
win|From Arabic "ayna" (where), worn down over the centuries. Egyptians say fein, Moroccans fin, Tunisians win — all the same word with the first sound changed.
waqtech|"Waqt" (time, from Classical Arabic waqt) + "ech" (what): "what time?" The -ech ending builds many Tunisian question words: kifech, 9adech, 3lech.
kifech|"Kif" (like, how) + "ech" (what): "like what?" Kif itself comes from Arabic "kayfa" (how). Egyptians say izzay, Moroccans kifech too.
3lech|"3la" (on, about) + "ech" (what): "on account of what?" = why. The same pattern as kifech and 9adech: an old preposition with the question ending -ech.
ena|Classical Arabic "ana" (I). Tunisians say it with a closed vowel, "ena", which is one of the small sounds that gives Tunisian its accent next to Egyptian or Gulf Arabic.
enti|Classical Arabic has "anta" for a man and "anti" for a woman; Tunisian kept just one, enti, for both. A Tunisian hallmark — in Egypt saying "enti" to a man would sound odd.
houwa|Classical Arabic "huwa" (he). The word that hides inside chnowa ("ech-houwa", what is it?).
hiya|Classical Arabic "hiya" (she). Hides inside chniya (ech-hiya, what is she?).
a7na|From Classical Arabic "nahnu" (we), reshaped in the Maghreb to a7na / 7na. The "we" of Algeria and Morocco as well.
houma|Classical Arabic "hum" (they) with an extra vowel. Tunisian, like most dialects, has one "they" for men and women; the classical language had two.
hedha|Classical Arabic "hadha" (this). The feminine is hedhi, the plural hedhouma — "these ones".
hedheka|"Hedha" (this) + "-ka", a far-pointing ending from Classical Arabic "dhalika". This near you, that over there.
3andi|"3and" (at, by) + "-i" (me): "at me" = I have. Arabic has no verb "to have" — you say something is "at" you. Every dialect does the same: Egyptian 3andi, Moroccan 3andi.
3andek|"3and" (at) + "-ek" (you): "at you" = you have. The same for a man or a woman in Tunisian.
3andou|"3and" (at) + "-ou" (him): "at him" = he has. The -ou ending is "his" wherever it appears: ktabou, his book.
w|Classical Arabic "wa" (and), one of the oldest words in the language and unchanged for 1,500 years. Written as a single letter, و.
wala|"Wa" (and) + "la" (no): "or". Also "nor" in "la … wala …", neither … nor. Classical Arabic uses "aw" for or; the dialects built their own.
ama|Classical Arabic "amma" (as for …) became the everyday "but" in Tunisian. Lekin is the other, more formal "but".
khater|From Arabic "khatir", the mind or heart; "3la khater" means "on account of". Shortened to khater, it does the job of "because". "Khatrek" also means "for your sake".
fi|Classical Arabic "fi" (in). Unchanged from the Quran to a Tunis café. Also at the heart of "fisa3" (quickly, "in the hour").
3la|Classical Arabic "3ala" (on, upon). Hides inside 3lech (why) and 3la kifek (as you like).
men|Classical Arabic "min" (from); also "than" when comparing: akbar men, bigger than.
l|Classical Arabic "li" (to, for). Sticks to the next word: lil-bit, to the room; lel-dar, to the house.
m3a|Classical Arabic "ma3a" (with). "M3ak" = with you, "m3aya" = with me. Rabbi m3ak: God be with you.
houni|From Classical Arabic "huna" (here). Algerians say hna, Moroccans hna; the Tunisian form keeps a second syllable.
ghadi|From Arabic "ghada", to go, so "where one goes" — over there. Also a Moroccan word, though there it means "going to" (the future).
tawa|From Classical Arabic "taww", "just now, at once". A Tunisian signature word: Egyptians say dilwa9ti, Moroccans daba, Algerians dork.
elyoum|"El" (the) + "youm" (day): "the day" = today. Classical Arabic says al-yawm — the same thing.
ghodwa|From Classical Arabic "ghadan" (tomorrow) and "ghudwa" (early morning). Moroccans say ghedda, Egyptians bokra, Tunisians ghodwa.
elbere7|From Arabic "al-bariha", "the past night", which came to mean yesterday. Egyptians say embare7, Moroccans lbare7 — the same word everywhere.
dima|From Arabic "da'iman" (always), from the root for lasting. Shared by every Maghreb dialect; Egyptians say dayman.
wa7ed|Classical Arabic "wahid" (one). Also "someone": wa7ed ja, someone came. Feminine wa7da.
zouz|From Arabic "zawj", a pair or a spouse — Tunisians count "a pair" where others say "two". Nearly all other dialects use "ithnin" (thnin); zouz marks Tunisia.
thletha|Classical Arabic "thalatha" (three). Tunisian keeps the "th" sound that Egyptian and Lebanese turned into "t" (talata).
arb3a|Classical Arabic "arba3a" (four). "Larb3a" is Wednesday, the fourth day.
khamsa|Classical Arabic "khamsa" (five). The hand of Fatima, the five-fingered charm against the evil eye, is also called khamsa.
setta|Classical Arabic "sitta" (six).
sab3a|Classical Arabic "sab3a" (seven). "Sbou3", the week, is seven days.
thmenya|Classical Arabic "thamaniya" (eight). Again the "th" that Tunisian keeps and Egyptian loses (tamanya).
tes3a|Classical Arabic "tis3a" (nine).
nhar|Classical Arabic "nahar" (daytime). Tunisians use it for the day as a span of time ("nhar twil", a long day); youm names the calendar day.
lil|Classical Arabic "layl" (night). "Alf layla wa layla" is the Arabian Nights. Lila is one particular night.
3am (year)|Classical Arabic "3am" (year). "Ras el 3am", the head of the year, is New Year. Sana is the formal word for the same thing.
rajel|Classical Arabic "rajul" (man). Also "husband": rajli, my husband. Plural rjel.
mra|From Classical Arabic "imra'a" (woman), shortened. Also "wife": marti, my wife. Plural nsa, from a different old root.
baba|A nursery word found in many languages — Arabic "baba", Turkish "baba", English "papa". Children's babbling gave the world its fathers.
ommi|"Omm" (mother, Classical Arabic umm) + "-i" (my). Tunisians use "ommi" even when speaking about someone else's mother. "Omm" is also Egypt's "Umm Kulthum".
khouya|"Khou" (brother, Classical Arabic akh) + "-ya" (my). Used to any man you like: "ya khouya", mate.
okht|Classical Arabic "ukht" (sister). "Okhti" = my sister, which, like khouya, can be any friendly woman.
weld|Classical Arabic "walad" (boy, child). "Wled" is the plural (children, boys); "weld" + a name = son of.
bent|Classical Arabic "bint" (girl, daughter). "Bnet" is the plural. Used to say "daughter of": bent 3ammi, my cousin.
ras|Classical Arabic "ra's" (head). Also "cape" on a map (Ras Angela, the top of Africa) and "start": ras el 3am, New Year.
3in|Classical Arabic "3ayn" (eye), which also means a spring of water, as in the Tunisian town names Ain Draham. The letter ع is named after it.
yed|Classical Arabic "yad" (hand). Tunisian uses it for the arm as well. The hand of Fatima is "yed Fatma".
dar|Classical Arabic "dar" (house, dwelling). Many Tunisian town names start with it: Dar Chaabane. Also "dar" the verb, to turn — different root, same letters.
beb|Classical Arabic "bab" (door, gate). The old gates of Tunis are all Bab something: Bab Bhar, the sea gate.
srir|Classical Arabic "sarir" (bed). Unchanged in sound for a thousand years.
chams|Classical Arabic "shams" (sun). "Ech-chams", the sun, with the sun-letter assimilation that this very word gives its name to.
b7ar|Classical Arabic "bahr" (sea). Bab Bhar in Tunis is the Sea Gate; "ba7ri" is anything from the sea, or the north wind.
ma|Classical Arabic "ma'" (water). The glottal stop at the end has gone, leaving a bare "ma". Plural "myeh".
9attous|From Latin "cattus" (cat) via Berber and Arabic "qitt". The Romans brought the cat and its name to North Africa; English "cat" is the same word.
kalb|Classical Arabic "kalb" (dog). The plural "kleb" is the usual insult. Also the name of the dog star, Kalb al-Akbar.
mekla|From "kla", to eat: a thing eaten, food. Arabic "akl" and "ma'kal", a place or thing of eating.
khobz|Classical Arabic "khubz" (bread). "Khabbaz" is the baker. Tunisian bread is the French baguette, but the word is ancient.
7lib|Classical Arabic "halib" (milk), from the root for milking.
blad|Classical Arabic "balad" (country, town). "Bledi", my hometown. English borrowed it from Algerian French as "the bled", the back of beyond.
souk|Classical Arabic "suq" (market). English and French took "souk" from the Maghreb; the covered souks of Tunis go back to the 1200s.
7anout|Classical Arabic "hanut" (shop, wine shop). Probably originally from Aramaic, the trading language of the ancient Near East.
mdrasa|Classical Arabic "madrasa" (school), from "darasa", to study. English uses "madrasa" for a religious school; in Tunisia it is any school.
mcha|Classical Arabic "masha" (to walk) came to mean "to go" in the Maghreb. "Mashi" = on foot; "ya mchi" = it works.
ja|Classical Arabic "ja'a" (to come). "Jey" = coming, next: el 3am ej jey, next year.
kla|Classical Arabic "akala" (to eat), with its first syllable dropped: akala → kla. "Yekol" = he eats; "mekla" = food.
chrab|Classical Arabic "shariba" (to drink). "Chorba" (soup) and "chrab" (a drink) share the root — soup is what you drink.
r9ad|Classical Arabic "raqada" (to lie down, sleep). Formal Arabic uses "nama" for sleep; Tunisians kept the lying-down verb.
khdem|Classical Arabic "khadama" (to serve) became "to work" in the Maghreb. "Khedma" = a job; "khadem" = a servant.
9ra|Classical Arabic "qara'a" (to read, recite). The word "Quran" is "the recitation" from the same root. Also "to study" in Tunisian.
chaf|Classical Arabic "shafa" (to look at, see). "Chouf!" = look! The usual Maghreb verb for seeing; Egyptians use the same.
9al|Classical Arabic "qala" (to say). "9olli" = tell me. "9awl" = a saying.
3mal|Classical Arabic "3amila" (to do, work). "3amel" = a worker; "ma3mal" = a factory. Tunisian uses it for both "do" and "make".
3ta|Classical Arabic "a3ta" (to give). "A3tini" = give me. "3ata" = a gift.
chra|Classical Arabic "ishtara" (to buy). "Chari" = a buyer; "chra w bi3", buying and selling.
jeb|Classical Arabic "ja'a bi", "to come with" something, melted into one verb: jeb, to bring. "Jib!" = bring it!
7ab|Classical Arabic "ahabba" (to love). Tunisian uses it for "want" too: n7eb nemchi, I want to go. "7obb" = love; "7bib" = darling.
3raf|Classical Arabic "3arafa" (to know, recognise). "Ma3rouf" = well known; "3arif" = an expert.
fhem|Classical Arabic "fahima" (to understand). "Mafhoum" = understood; "fahem" = someone who gets it.
l3ab|Classical Arabic "la3iba" (to play). "La3eb" = a player; "lo3ba" = a game or toy.
behi|From Classical Arabic "bahi", splendid, radiant. Only Tunisians use it as the everyday "good, OK" — a one-word passport. Algerians say mli7, Moroccans mezyen.
khayeb|From Arabic "kha'ib", disappointed, failed. In Tunisian it covers ugly, bad and nasty: the opposite of behi. "Mouch khayeb" = not bad.
kbir|Classical Arabic "kabir" (big, great). "Akbar" = bigger, as in Allahu akbar, God is greatest. Also "old" for people.
sghir|Classical Arabic "saghir" (small, young). "Asghar" = smaller. The opposite pair kbir/sghir does for both size and age.
jdid|Classical Arabic "jadid" (new). "Jdid" is also the slang for "cool" among young Tunisians.
far7an|Classical Arabic "farhan" (joyful), from "farah", joy. "Far7" also means a wedding party.
ta3ban|Classical Arabic "ta3ban" (tired), from "ta3ab", weariness. Also "run down, unwell" when said of a sick person.
mridh|Classical Arabic "marid" (ill). "Mardh" = illness; "mmarrdha" = a nurse, the one with the sick.

ahla|Short for "ahlan wa sahlan", the ancient Arab welcome: "(you have come to) family and easy ground". Used as a casual hi across the Arab world.
sba7 el khir|"Morning of goodness". Classical Arabic "sabah al-khayr". The reply is "sba7 en nour", morning of light.
msa el khir|"Evening of goodness". Classical Arabic "masa' al-khayr". Reply: "msa en nour", evening of light.
tesba7 3la khir|"May you wake up in goodness": a wish for the morning, said at night. "Tesba7" is from sba7, to become morning.
inshallah|"In sha' Allah", "if God wills", said of anything in the future. Spanish "ojalá" (hopefully) comes from this Arabic phrase.
labes|"La bas", "no harm". The Maghreb's "fine, OK": "labes?" asks how you are and answers it. Egyptians say "kwayyes" instead.
9adech|"9add" (amount, size) + "ech" (what): "what amount?" = how much. Also "9addech el wa9t", what time is it.
entouma|"Enti" (you) + "-ouma" (plural ending, as in houma): you all. Classical Arabic has antum and antunna; Tunisian has one word.
lekin|Classical Arabic "lakin" (but). The more formal of Tunisian's two "buts"; ama is the everyday one.
bech|From Arabic "bi-ash", "with what" or "in order to", which became the marker of the future: bech nemchi, I'm going to go. A Maghreb invention; Egyptians say "7a-" instead.
ken|Classical Arabic "kana" (he was) became "if" in Tunisian: ken ji, if you come. The same word means "was" when it stands as a verb.
kif|Classical Arabic "kayfa" (how) became "like, as" and "when": kif ma t7eb, as you like; kif ja, when he came. "Kif kif" = the same.
bla|Classical Arabic "bila" (without): "bla sokker", without sugar. "Blech" = for nothing, free.
9bal|Classical Arabic "qabla" (before). "9bal ma" = before (doing); "men 9bal" = beforehand.
ba3d|Classical Arabic "ba3da" (after). "Ba3d ghodwa" = the day after tomorrow.
mba3d|"Men" (from) + "ba3d" (after) squashed together: afterwards, later. A Tunisian form; Egyptians say ba3dein.
fou9|Classical Arabic "fawqa" (above, over). "El fou9" = upstairs.
ta7t|Classical Arabic "tahta" (under). "El ta7t" = downstairs.
9oddem|Classical Arabic "quddam" (in front of). "9addem" = to present, to put in front.
barcha|A Tunisian-only word for "a lot" — the first word visitors learn. Its origin is debated: possibly from Arabic "bi-rash", or from Italian "barca", a boatload. Algerians say bezzef, Moroccans bezzaf.
chwaya|From Arabic "shay'" (thing) in its diminutive "shuwayya", a little thing. "Chwaya chwaya" = little by little. Shared with Egypt (shwayya).
bark|A Tunisian word for "only, just": "wa7ed bark", just one. Thought to be from Berber; Algerians use it too. Moroccans say "ghir".
zeda|From Arabic "ziyada" (an addition, more): "also, too". "Zid" = add, carry on. "Zeda" as "too" is Tunisian; Egyptians say kaman.
ey|Tunisian "yes". Not Classical Arabic "na3am" at all; probably from Berber or an old Arabic "ay". Egyptians say aywa, Moroccans wah.
le|Classical Arabic "la" (no). Tunisians say it with a closed vowel, "le", which can surprise other Arabs.
mouch|From "ma" (not) + "ech" (thing): "not a thing". Negates nouns and adjectives: mouch behi, not good. Moroccans say machi, Egyptians mish.
famma|From "fi" (in) + "ma" (what): "there is". Algerians say kayen, Moroccans kayn, Egyptians fih — famma is Tunisia's own.
mafammech|"Ma" + famma + "-ch": the not-sandwich around "there is". The "-ch" comes from Arabic "shay'", thing — "not a thing".
kol|Classical Arabic "kull" (all, every). "Kol youm" = every day; "el kol" = everyone.
7aja|Classical Arabic "haja" (a need) came to mean "a thing". "7aja khayba" = a bad thing. Egyptians use the same word.
yezzi|A Tunisian "enough!": from Arabic "yajzi", "it suffices". "Yezzi fik" = that's enough of you.
heya|"Hayya", Classical Arabic for "come on!", as in the call to prayer: hayya 3ala s-salah. "Heya bina" = let's go.
3achra|Classical Arabic "3ashara" (ten). The -ach ending of the teens (7dach, thnach) is this word worn down.
ethnin|"El ithnayn", "the second (day)": Monday. Arabic weekdays are numbered from Sunday, the first.
jem3a|Classical Arabic "jum3a", "the gathering": Friday, the day of congregation. Also "week" in Tunisian, from one Friday to the next.
sebt|Classical Arabic "sabt" (Saturday), from Hebrew "shabbat", the Sabbath. The same word as Spanish sábado.
a7ad|"El a7ad", "the first (day)": Sunday. "A7ad" also means "one" or "someone" in Classical Arabic.
sa3a|Classical Arabic "sa3a" (hour). Also a watch or clock. "9addech es-sa3a?" = what time is it?
chta|Classical Arabic "shita'" (winter). In Tunisian it also means rain — winter is when it rains.
tfol|Classical Arabic "tifl" (child, infant). In Tunisian it is a boy; "tofla" a girl, "sghar" the children.
tofla|The feminine of tfol: a girl. Classical Arabic "tifla".
sa7eb|Classical Arabic "sahib" (companion, owner). "Ya sa7bi" = mate. Also the owner: sa7eb el 7anout, the shopkeeper. Feminine sa7ba.
wjeh|Classical Arabic "wajh" (face). "Wjeh" also means "direction"; "mwajjeh" = heading.
wdhen|Classical Arabic "udhun" (ear). Plural "wdhnin".
khchem|Tunisian for nose; Classical Arabic says "anf". Khchem comes from "khaysham", the nostrils. Egyptians say manakhir, Moroccans nif.
bit|Classical Arabic "bayt" (house). In the Maghreb it shrank to a room: bit en-noum, the bedroom. The house is dar.
koujina|From Italian "cucina" (kitchen). Italians were Tunisia's biggest European community before 1956, and the kitchen kept their word.
chobbek|From Persian "shubbak" via Arabic: a lattice window. Egyptians say the same.
korsi|Classical Arabic "kursi" (chair, throne). The Throne Verse of the Quran is Ayat al-Kursi.
tilifoun|French "téléphone". The t-l-f-n consonants have given Arabic a verb too: tsel, to phone.
ktab|Classical Arabic "kitab" (book), from "kataba", to write. "Maktba" = library, "kateb" = writer, "mektab" = office: one root, a family.
flous|Classical Arabic "fulus", plural of "fals", a small copper coin — from Latin "follis", a bag of coins, which also gave "fils" in the Gulf.
9mar|Classical Arabic "qamar" (moon). "9amra" = moonlight; a beautiful face is "kif el 9mar", like the moon.
sma|Classical Arabic "sama'" (sky, heaven). "Sma" with the final glottal stop gone.
skhoun|Classical Arabic "sakhin" (hot). "Sakhkhan" = to heat up. Tunisian uses skhoun for hot things; 7ar for hot weather or spicy food.
bared|Classical Arabic "barid" (cold). "Bard" = the cold; "barrad" = a fridge or a teapot.
7san|Classical Arabic "hisan" (horse). Egyptians say the same; Moroccans say 3awd.
tir|Classical Arabic "ta'ir" (bird, flying thing), from "tara", to fly. "Tayara" = aeroplane; "mtar" = airport.
7out|Classical Arabic "hut" means a whale or a big fish (Jonah's); the Maghreb uses it for any fish. Egyptians say samak.
bidh|Classical Arabic "bayd" (eggs). "Bidha" = one egg; "abyadh" = white — eggs are the white things.
l7am|Classical Arabic "lahm" (meat). Bethlehem, "Bayt Lahm", is the house of meat (or bread, in Hebrew).
khodhra|Classical Arabic "khudra" (greenery, vegetables), from "akhdhar", green. "Khadhdhar" = greengrocer.
ghalla|Classical Arabic "ghalla", a harvest or crop. In Tunisian the word moved to mean fruit. Elsewhere "fakiha" is fruit.
tfe7|Classical Arabic "tuffah" (apples). "Tfe7a" is a single apple.
9ahwa|Classical Arabic "qahwa", coffee — the word that travelled through Turkish "kahve" to Italian "caffè" and English "coffee". Also a café.
tey|From Chinese "te" via Dutch and French "thé". Arabic "shay" took the other Chinese form, "cha", through Persia — Tunisians took the sea route word.
7lou|Classical Arabic "hulw" (sweet). In Tunisian also pretty, cute: "bent 7lowa", a lovely girl. "7lawa" = sweets.
ji3an|Classical Arabic "jaw3an" (hungry), from "ju3", hunger.
3atchan|Classical Arabic "3atshan" (thirsty), from "3atash", thirst.
7wayej|Plural of 7aja (thing): "things", which came to mean clothes. "7wayji" = my clothes. Egyptians say hidoum.
mdina|Classical Arabic "madina" (city). In the Maghreb it also names the old walled town: the Medina of Tunis is a World Heritage site. Medina in Arabia is "The City".
chera3|Classical Arabic "shari3" (street, highway). Also "law" in the religious sense — the same word.
thniya|Tunisian for a road: from Arabic "thaniya", a mountain pass or a way between. "9ta3 eth-thniya" = cross the road. Elsewhere tari9.
blasa|From French "place" or Spanish "plaza": a place or a seat. "Blasa fadhya" = a free seat. A European word gone fully Tunisian.
karhba|From Arabic "kahraba'" (electricity), from Persian "kahruba", amber — which sparks when rubbed. A car was "the electric thing"; Egyptians say 3arabiya, Moroccans tomobil.
rja3|Classical Arabic "raja3a" (to return). "Rja3t" = I came back.
rawwa7|From "rou7" (soul, self): to take oneself home. "Rawwa7!" = go home. A Maghreb verb; the word also gives "ra7a", rest.
9am|Classical Arabic "qama" (to stand up, rise). "9ayyem" = to lift or put away; "9iyama" = resurrection.
9a3ad|Classical Arabic "qa3ada" (to sit). In Tunisian also "to stay": 9a3adt fi dar, I stayed at home. "9a3ida" = a rule, a base.
kteb|Classical Arabic "kataba" (to write). The k-t-b root gives book, office, library, writer and clerk.
t3allem|Classical Arabic "ta3allama" (to learn), the reflexive of 3allem, to teach: to get oneself taught. "3ilm" = knowledge.
sma3|Classical Arabic "sami3a" (to hear). "Esma3!" = listen! "Sma3" is also the name of Sufi listening music.
7ka|Classical Arabic "haka" (to tell, narrate). "7kaya" = a story; "chnia el 7kaya?" = what's the story? Tunisian uses it for talking in general.
khdha|Classical Arabic "akhadha" (to take), with the first syllable dropped like kla. "Khoudh!" = take it!
najjem|A Tunisian "can": from "najm", a star — perhaps "to find one's star", or from "najama", to result. "Ma nejjemch" = I can't. Egyptians say 9ader, Moroccans 9der.
lezem|Classical Arabic "lazima" (to be necessary). "Lezem nemchi" = I must go. Also "mouch lezem", no need.
twil|Classical Arabic "tawil" (long, tall). "Tol" = length; "3la tol" = straight on.
9sir|Classical Arabic "qasir" (short). "9assar" = to shorten.
9wi|Classical Arabic "qawiy" (strong). "9owwa" = strength.
9dim|Classical Arabic "qadim" (old, ancient). For things only; an old person is kbir. "9adim" also means "coming" — a different word.
ndhif|Classical Arabic "nadhif" (clean). "Nadhdhaf" = to clean.

s3ib|Classical Arabic "sa3b" (difficult). "So3ouba" = difficulty.

ghali|Classical Arabic "ghali" (dear, expensive). Also "precious": "ghali 3liya", dear to me.
rkhis|Classical Arabic "rakhis" (cheap). "Arkhas" = cheaper.
kelma|Classical Arabic "kalima" (word). "Klem" = talk, words; "tkallem" = to speak.

3al akther|"3la" (on) + "akther" (more): at most.

belkol|"Bi" (with) + "el kol" (the all): completely; with a negative, at all.

akahaw|A Tunisian-only "that's all": from "aka" (that) + "hou" (it) — "just that". Said to end a list or a story.

ba3d ma|"Ba3d" (after) + "ma" (that): after doing.

wa9t ma|"Wa9t" (time) + "ma" (that): whenever.
7atta ken|"7atta" (even) + "ken" (if): even if.

7asb|Classical Arabic "hasab" (according to), from 7aseb, to reckon.

3and|Classical Arabic "3inda" (at, by, with). "3andi" = I have; "3and Ali" = at Ali's.
mte3|From Arabic "mata3" (goods, property): of, belonging to. "El dar mte3 Ali" = Ali's house. Tunisian and Libyan; Moroccans say dyal.
rou7i|"Rou7" (soul) + "-i" (my): myself. Arabic has no "self" word, so it uses the soul.
rou7ek|"Rou7" (soul) + "-ek" (your): yourself. "Chadd rou7ek" = hold yourself together.
rou7ou|"Rou7" (soul) + "-ou" (his): himself.

9adhya|Classical Arabic "qadiya" (matter, case), from "qada", to judge. "9adhi" = a judge.

chbik|"Ech" (what) + "bik" (in you): what is in you? What's wrong, what's up. "Chbih?" = what's up with him?
3andek el 7a9|"You have the truth": you're right. 7a9 is truth and a right at once.
ma 3andich khbar|"I have no news": I've no idea. Khbar is news, information.
rabbi ysahhel|"May my Lord make (it) easy", from sehel, easy. Said to anyone setting out on something.
rabbi m3ak|"My Lord (is) with you": good luck, and a goodbye. Tunisians say rabbi where others say Allah.
sa7it|"Your health", from sa7a: thanks, especially for small favours. Also "cheers". The Algerian "sa7a" is the same.
3la kifek|"On your taste": as you like. Kif here is pleasure, from Arabic "kayf" — the same word as "kif", the hashish of the Rif.
kif kif|"Like like": the same. The French army brought "kif-kif" home from Algeria and it is still French slang.
nharek zin|"Your day (is) lovely": have a nice day. Zin is beauty, from the same root as mezyen.
t3ich|"May you live", from 3ach: thanks. A shorter 3aychek. The reply is "w enti" — and you.
allah ybarek|"God blesses": said in admiration so the evil eye does not follow. Praise without it can sound like envy.
machallah|"What God has willed": wonderful. Said of a child, a house, anything good — again to ward off the eye.
ma3lich|"Nothing on it" (ma 3alayhi shay'): never mind, no harm done. Heard across the whole Arab world.
mouch mouchkla|"Not a problem". Mochkla from Arabic "mushkila".
3la rasi|"On my head": with pleasure — I carry your request on my head. The old way of saying "at your service".
3la 3ini|"On my eye": gladly. Like 3la rasi: your wish sits on something precious.
rabbi y7afdhek|"May my Lord keep you", from 7fadh: thank you, bless you.
taw taw|"Now now", from tawa: right away, or just a moment ago. Tunisian only.
ya 7asra|"Oh sorrow": what a pity — and "those were the days". The Tunisian sigh for the past. 7asra is grief for what is lost.
ya khsara|"Oh loss", from khsar: what a shame.
ya latif|"Oh Gentle One": Al-Latif is one of the 99 names of God. Said when something shocks you.
ya rabbi|"Oh my Lord": oh dear, oh God. The everyday sigh.
3la sa7tek|"To your health": cheers, when drinking. Sa7a is health.
rabbi ykhallik|"May my Lord keep you", from khalla: please — and thank you. Said when asking a favour.

marra marra|"A time a time": now and then.
